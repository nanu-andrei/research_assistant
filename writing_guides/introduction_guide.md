# Academic Writing Guide: Introduction Chapter (IEEE Standard)

## Objective
The Introduction establishes the context, necessity, and novelty of the research. It must convince the reviewer within the first two pages of the problem's importance and the superiority of the proposed approach.

## Industry Standard 5-Part Narrative Structure
1. **The Hook & Broad Context (¶1):**
   - High-level overview of the domain (e.g., contactless sensing, ubiquitous WiFi infrastructure).
   - Real-world impact and practical relevance across smart environments, healthcare, and surveillance.
2. **The Problem & Critical Gap (¶2):**
   - Why existing solutions fall short (e.g., multipath distortion, lack of cross-domain transferability, privacy vulnerabilities).
   - High-weight citations to the top-ranked foundational papers from Phase 2.
3. **Core Insight & Proposed Approach (¶3):**
   - The central conceptual breakthrough (e.g., self-supervised contrastive learning on raw Channel State Information).
   - Why this insight circumvents prior limitations.
4. **Summary of Key Contributions (Bulleted List):**
   - Explicit, quantifiable contributions:
     - *Novel Architecture/Algorithm:* Formal design of the sensing pipeline.
     - *Empirical Validation:* Benchmark evaluation across diverse physical environments.
     - *Open Release / Framework:* Open-access tooling or reproducible datasets.
5. **Paper Roadmap (Closing Paragraph):**
   - Standard organization overview: *"The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II surveys related work..."*

## Weighting Rules
- Papers ranked #1 and #2 in Phase 2 Introductions must be cited as primary motivations and benchmark targets.
- Explicitly contrast the proposed work against the #1 ranked problem framing.
